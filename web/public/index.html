01 / 04 — THE MANUSCRIPT

Every reference starts as a line of text.

Typed, pasted, inherited from a co-author, or invented by a model. On the page they all look the same.

02 / 04 — THE PARSE

Split into the five fields that can be checked.

Title, authors, year, venue, DOI. No model reads the manuscript. A parser does, deterministically.

03 / 04 — THE REGISTRY

Compared field by field against the scholarly record.

OpenAlex and the DOI handle registry. Does the paper exist, do these fields belong to it, and has it been retracted?

04 / 04 — THE VERDICT

Stamped. Never guessed.

A network failure is “cannot certify”, never a pass. A fabrication verdict needs a 404 at two registries. Anything unclear goes to review.

Seven verdicts, three tiers.

Fail-closed means the tool would rather send you to check a reference by hand than let a bad one through. Nothing unrecognised is ever routed to “pass”.

Fabricated candidate
Blocking. The DOI is registered nowhere, checked at both OpenAlex and doi.org.
Inconsistent
Blocking. The paper exists, but the title, authors, year, venue or DOI do not belong to it. Field-mixing lives here.
Retracted
Blocking. The registry marks the work as retracted.
Benign drift
Review. Formatting or online-first versus print differences only.
Unverified
Review. Found nowhere. Not evidence of fabrication; check by hand.
Registry unreachable
Review. A network problem. The audit cannot certify, so it says so.
Consistent
Pass. Every field matches the registry record.

RefGuard verifies that references describe real papers accurately. It cannot judge whether they are the right papers to cite.
