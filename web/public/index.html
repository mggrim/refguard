01 / 04   THE MANUSCRIPT

A reference starts as a line of text.

Whether you typed the reference, a co-author sent it or a model invented it, the line looks the same on the page.

02 / 04   THE PARSE

The parser splits the line into fields.

RefGuard extracts the title, authors, year, venue and DOI with fixed rules, and it reads your manuscript without a language model.

03 / 04   THE REGISTRY

RefGuard compares the fields with the scholarly record.

RefGuard asks Crossref and the DOI registry three questions: does the paper exist, do the cited fields belong to that paper, and is the paper retracted? For books, it asks Open Library, because the article registries index journals and most of the monographs you cite predate DOIs.

04 / 04   THE VERDICT

RefGuard bases its verdicts on evidence.

When a registry is unreachable, RefGuard answers “cannot certify” and withholds a pass. It calls a reference fabricated only when both registries return 404, and it marks unclear references for review.

The verdicts and their tiers.

Fail-closed means that RefGuard returns uncertain references to you to check by hand and withholds a pass. A reference passes only when the registries recognize it.

Fabricated candidate
Blocking. Both Crossref and doi.org return 404 for the DOI.
Inconsistent
Blocking. The paper exists, but the cited title, authors, year, venue or DOI conflict with the paper's record. RefGuard gives this verdict to references that mix fields from two papers.
Retracted
Blocking. The registry marks the paper as retracted.
Benign drift
Review. RefGuard found the right record with a small difference: a citation of the online-first year of a paper that has since appeared in print, a year off by one for a reason other than the gap between online and print dates, a changed subtitle, or an incomplete author list. The print year is the published state.
Unverified
Review. The registries left the reference unconfirmed: the search drew a blank or found only a different work, which the note names. An unconfirmed reference is a weaker finding than a fabricated one, so check it by hand. RefGuard checks books against Open Library. Theses, reports and trade press are unindexed, and the report says so, so you know the reason for the verdict.
Ambiguous match
Review. RefGuard found a record, but without a DOI to anchor the match, the record may be the wrong one: the title is too general and the record's authors all differ from yours, or the record's year differs from yours by two or more years. Most likely RefGuard matched a different work or edition, so your reference itself may well be accurate; check which work or edition you cited.
Registry unreachable
Review. RefGuard lost its connection to the registry and so leaves the reference uncertified.
Consistent
Pass. The cited fields all match the registry record.

Check a bibliography

RefGuard checks whether your references accurately describe real papers; the choice of the right paper to cite remains your judgment. RefGuard draws on verification work by Katie Apker, Pedro Sant’Anna, Scott Cunningham, and Dhuliawala et al. (2023, Chain-of-Verification). RefGuard takes its registry data from Crossref, which publishes the Retraction Watch database, from Open Library for books, and from doi.org.
