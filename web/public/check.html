Four levels of attention

Results are grouped by whether they need your time. Fix this: likely a real error, such as fields from two different papers or a retracted work. Worth a look: a real difference you may want to change, such as citing the online-first year of a paper that now has a volume and issue. Probably fine: a difference of record rather than of reference, such as a reprint, a later edition or a handbook chapter's print and online years. Can't check here: a source no registry indexes, or one the registries do not hold; not finding it is not evidence of a problem.

Eight verdicts in three tiers

Fail-closed means an uncertain reference is sent back to you rather than passed. Nothing the registries do not recognise ever passes.

Fabricated candidate
Blocking. Both Crossref and doi.org return 404 for the DOI.
Inconsistent
Blocking. The paper exists, but the title, authors, year, venue or DOI do not match it. References that mix fields from two papers land here.
Retracted
Blocking. The registry marks the work as retracted.
Benign drift
Review. A small difference on the right record: the online-first year cited where the paper has a print year, a year one off that neither date explains, a changed subtitle, or an incomplete author list.
Ambiguous match
Review. A record was found without a DOI to anchor it, but it may be the wrong one: no author matches, or its year is two or more away from yours. Check which record or edition you cited.
Unverified
Review. Nothing confirmed it. Not proof of fabrication. If the search returned only a different work, the note names it. Books, theses, reports and trade press are not indexed at all, and the note says so.
Registry unreachable
Review. A network problem stopped the check, so the reference cannot be certified.
Consistent
Pass. Every field matches the registry record.

RefGuard checks whether each reference accurately describes a real paper. It cannot judge whether that paper is the right one to cite. Registry data from Crossref, Open Library and doi.org.
